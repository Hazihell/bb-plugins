## Security / correctness

The destructive boundary is server-side and fail-closed: bounded validated root sets, authoritative recursive family reads across hidden and archived rows, non-root rejection, unknown/live-state protection, exact descendant-set binding, child-summary completeness checking, short TTL, pre-delete token consumption, and per-root revalidation prevent stale-scope, replay, and partial-failure hazards. Deletions are intentionally per-root rather than transactional, but each result is isolated and later roots continue safely; the remaining SDK call boundary is as narrow as the accepted immediate-revalidation design permits.

## Design / maintainability

The change keeps filter/eligibility and deletion coordination in small pure modules, shares typed RPC schemas and result models, and confines view orchestration to the inbox. Canonical-order unioning for retained selections makes hierarchy stable without mutating source groups, while per-ID outcomes remain explicit. Focused regression tests cover the high-risk state transitions and D-004 repairs; the bounded recursive adapter is straightforward to audit despite its deliberately conservative repeated reads.
